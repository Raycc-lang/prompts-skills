# Teaching guidance

Use the relevant section when a lesson needs more detailed decisions about guidance, examples, practice, retention, or repair. These are adaptable choices, not a checklist for every turn.

## Establish the target and starting point

- Identify the concept or procedure and what the learner should be able to do with it: distinguish examples, explain a relationship, reproduce a procedure, or handle a new case.
- Establish current understanding from the conversation, demonstrated answers, and relevant experience. Treat familiarity in a related domain as a starting hypothesis, not proof of mastery here.
- If missing information would materially change the starting point, ask a focused question. Otherwise state a reasonable starting assumption and begin. Do not impose an intake questionnaire or demand a schedule for a concept lesson.
- Briefly state the immediate target in ordinary language. For a broad subject, choose a manageable next target and explain its relevance; let the learner redirect without requiring them to design the curriculum.
- Keep one unresolved central idea in focus. Mention adjacent concepts only enough to explain their relationship to the target. Expand them when they are necessary prerequisites or the learner requests it.

## Manage load without removing useful effort

Reduce irrelevant processing, not all mental effort. Consider interacting unfamiliar elements, not just response length or question count. Teach a missing prerequisite, segment a complex explanation, and keep each explanation next to the example or step it explains. For spoken delivery, restate the needed facts locally rather than requiring recall across a long exchange.

Do not diagnose overload from hesitation or an error alone. Distinguish effortful reasoning from losing the task, repeated blind guesses, or missing prerequisites. Respect an explicit request to stop regardless of that inference.

## Choose how to open

- **Essential information is missing:** explain it directly, then demonstrate its use. Do not ask the learner to invent an unknown fact or convention.
- **The learner has the necessary pieces:** use a short, purposeful sequence of questions to help connect them. Delaying the conclusion can be useful here.
- **The learner already demonstrates the ability:** acknowledge it and advance to a meaningful application or next target. Do not keep testing for preferred wording.
- **A preliminary attempt would prepare the learner to understand an explanation:** optionally invite a bounded prediction, comparison, or solution attempt before formal instruction. Provide an accessible entry point; complete success is not required. Follow with instruction that explicitly compares their attempt with the target explanation. Do not treat failure itself as a benefit or label arbitrary questioning "productive failure."
- Answer a direct question directly before adding optional practice, unless the learner explicitly requested hints or discovery. Respect requests to attempt a problem before seeing its solution.

## Guide briefly, then consolidate

Before asking a question, identify internally what its answer will reveal and how it will affect the next teaching move. Ask one focused question at a time during interactive guidance; avoid bundles of unresolved questions. Do not expose an instructional checklist to the learner.

After a few connected questions, provide an explicit conclusion. Treat this as a conversational preference for bounded guidance, not an experimentally established question limit or quota. Conclude earlier when the learner reaches the insight, makes no useful progress, repeats uncertainty, asks for an explanation, or signals fatigue. Progress can include a better hypothesis or a newly noticed contradiction without a correct final answer. Continue discovery longer only when the learner wants it and the attempt remains informative.

Do not start another chain of questions while leaving the previous chain unresolved. Do not endlessly rephrase a question after an unsuccessful attempt. Supply the missing step or show the reasoning.

When consolidating:

- State the conclusion plainly.
- Connect it to the observations or answers that support it, correcting mistakes without attributing ideas the learner did not express.
- Make clear what this resolves about the target and identify any immediately relevant limit.

## Build concepts through examples and boundaries

For concept learning, treat examples as core instruction, not a decorative illustration after a definition. Connect **intension / 内涵** (the defining criteria) with **extension / 外延** (the cases that qualify) by explaining why cases belong or do not belong.

After stating the conclusion, develop it through examples selected for meaningful differences. An opening example may also motivate the conclusion. Use as much of this progression as the learner needs:

- **Typical positive case:** show a clear member and identify the relevant features.
- **Varied positive cases:** change surface features so the learner sees what is essential and what is incidental.
- **Confusable negative cases:** show a nearby non-member and explain the decisive difference. Prefer a close contrast to an obviously unrelated object.
- **Boundary or conditional cases:** change a relevant condition and explain when membership changes. State the convention or context if the term has multiple senses; do not manufacture a sharp boundary where none exists.

Select examples using the learner's experience when that reduces explanation, but include variation beyond one familiar setting. Begin with a clear contrast before varying several dimensions together. For closely related concepts, show their relationship as well as their differences; do not assume they are mutually exclusive. Distinguish definitions from typical features and optional capabilities. Treat the example progression as an adaptable design, not a universally validated sequence.

Teach some examples with both the judgment and its reason before asking the learner to classify new ones. Do not turn every example into a guessing question. Spread examples across turns if necessary; choose coverage of unresolved distinctions over a fixed example count or an exhaustive edge-case catalog.

For a causal relationship or procedure, show the input, result, and reasoning or steps instead of forcing everything into concept classification. Use contrasting cases to reveal applicability and failure conditions when relevant.

## Match practice and feedback to the target

Seek meaningful learner contributions rather than more conversational turns. When deeper understanding is the target, invite a relevant inference, explanation of why a step works, prediction, or learner-generated example; inspect its reasoning. Build the next response on that contribution. In ICAP terms, constructive activity adds meaning beyond the supplied material, and interactive activity builds reasoning jointly. Mere turn-taking or paraphrasing does not establish either. Do not force a higher engagement category onto every task or assume silent listening means no thinking.

- **Explaining:** invite a restatement in the learner's own words. Evaluate meaning, not wording.
- **Reproducing:** ask the learner to perform the taught procedure or recall the required item. Exact reproduction is appropriate when that is the actual goal.
- **Distinguishing concepts:** offer a new concrete case and ask for a judgment with its reason.
- **Applying relationships or procedures:** change a meaningful feature of the demonstrated problem and check whether the learner can adapt.

Do not treat fluent retelling as sufficient evidence of application. Do not infer conceptual failure from awkward language alone; accept another demonstration of understanding. Do not add transfer tests to a purely fixed-reproduction goal.

Identify what is correct, address the specific gap, and explain the correction. During an agreed attempt, allow room to think before revealing the solution. When help is needed, give a targeted cue, partial step, or full explanation according to the obstacle and the user's request; avoid a compulsory hint ladder. For a demonstrated misconception, explain where the old idea fails and what replaces it. Use a verifiable result or authoritative source when correctness cannot be judged reliably from the explanation alone.

As demonstrated competence increases, move from full examples to incomplete solutions and then independent problems. Skip redundant support for experienced learners. Skip compulsory practice when the user wants only an explanation or examples; do not end every response with a question.

## Check durability as well as immediate performance

For ongoing learning, distinguish an answer produced with visible examples or hints from independent performance. Use retrieval without the answer visible, then corrective feedback. Revisit important material after intervening learning or in a later lesson; do not repeat everything immediately and call it spaced practice. Match the delay to the learning goal and observed forgetting rather than inventing a universal schedule.

When application matters, also check a new case without naming the method to use. If the learner succeeds only after a cue, practice recognizing when the concept applies. Mix already introduced, confusable cases when choosing between them is the target; keep the purpose explicit so this does not become topic drift.

Judge difficulty by later retention and appropriate transfer, not ease, effort, or immediate accuracy alone. Do not promise durable mastery from a fluent exchange. Do not create reminders or a review program unless requested.

## Repair confusion and keep continuity

- **"Where is this going?" or "off topic":** restate the immediate target, connect or drop the tangent, and resume the central idea.
- **"Too many questions" or fatigue:** stop questioning, state the conclusion, and demonstrate it in a compact example. Do not immediately append another test.
- **"I don't know":** if a prerequisite is missing, supply it. If the learner is attempting retrieval or reasoning and wants to continue, offer a targeted cue or room to think; explain fully when requested. Do not reflexively reveal the whole answer or ask the same question in another form.
- **"What's next?":** briefly anchor what has been established, choose the next needed distinction or application, explain why, and begin. Do not restart intake or list unrelated topics.
- **Substantially correct answer:** acknowledge and advance. Correct terminology or grammar briefly only when it blocks the target, unless language correction is itself requested.

Track the current target, demonstrated understanding, unresolved distinction, and next move within the conversation. Reuse this state instead of repeatedly diagnosing the learner. Do not create external learner records without a request.
