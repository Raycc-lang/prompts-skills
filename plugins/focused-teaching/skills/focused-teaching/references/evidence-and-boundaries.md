# Evidence and boundaries

Read when explaining the skill's research basis, resolving a disputed teaching choice, or revising its rules. These sources support conditional principles, not validation of this complete AI teaching workflow. Separate findings from implementation choices. Do not load or recite this reference during ordinary lessons.

## Cognitive Load Theory

Sweller, van Merriënboer, and Paas (2019), [Cognitive Architecture and Instructional Design: 20 Years Later](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10648-019-09465-5).

Relevant sections: worked examples, split attention, variability, expertise reversal, guidance fading, transient information. Support must fit prior knowledge and material complexity. The skill's chunking and support adjustments apply these principles; conversational signs are imperfect clues, not measurements of working-memory load.

## Desirable difficulties

Bjork and Bjork (2011), [Making Things Hard on Yourself, But in a Good Way](https://bjorklab.psych.ucla.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/13/2016/04/EBjork_RBjork_2011.pdf).

Short-term performance can misrepresent durable learning. Retrieval, spacing, and variation can be beneficial under suitable conditions. Difficulty without the resources needed to benefit is not desirable. Do not infer that frustration, delayed answers, or random topic switching improve learning. The skill's bounded guidance is a user-informed interaction choice, not a dosage established by this source.

## ICAP and constructivist learning

Chi and Wylie (2014), [The ICAP Framework: Linking Cognitive Engagement to Active Learning Outcomes](https://education.asu.edu/sites/g/files/litvpz656/files/lcl/chiwylie2014icap_2.pdf).

Classify engagement by what learners do with content. Constructive contributions go beyond given information; interactive engagement requires substantive reciprocal construction. Activity labels and conversational turn counts are insufficient. The paper identifies caveats to its ordering and distinguishes building understanding from discovering facts. Use it to design learner contributions, not to reject explanation or mechanically maximize interactivity. Extending these criteria to an AI tutor is an application, not proof of equivalent outcomes.

## Problem solving before instruction

Sinha and Kapur (2021), [When Problem Solving Followed by Instruction Works: Evidence for Productive Failure](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/00346543211019105).

The meta-analysis supports benefits under particular learner and design conditions, especially for conceptual learning and transfer. Productive failure includes later instruction built on learner-generated attempts. It is not unstructured struggle, and an initially wrong solution need not mean an unsuccessful learning design. Do not transplant classroom treatment lengths into chat or claim every preliminary prediction implements productive failure.

## Retrieval and delayed assessment

Karpicke and Blunt (2011), [Retrieval Practice Produces More Learning than Elaborative Studying with Concept Mapping](https://learninglab.psych.purdue.edu/downloads/2011/2011_Karpicke_Blunt_Science.pdf).

In the studied science-text tasks, retrieval practice improved delayed performance, including inference questions. This supports checking learning beyond visible-answer fluency. It does not establish that concept maps are universally inferior or that all practical abilities can be assessed by verbal recall. Match verification to the target and distinguish assessment from additional teaching.

## Limits of this synthesis

Use varied examples and close contrasts to serve a specific unresolved distinction. The exact order of positive cases, non-examples, and boundary cases is a design heuristic, not an experimentally established universal recipe. Likewise, one central focus, a short questioning stretch, and local conclusions implement this learner's stated needs. Keep them adaptable without reviving aimless questioning.

Theory alignment and simulated teaching checks do not establish effectiveness for this learner. Revise from observed independent performance, retained understanding, transfer where relevant, and learner feedback about pacing. Do not diagnose a learner from a single hesitant answer.
